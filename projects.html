Research and Data Science

One of my main academic interests is understanding how data can be used to investigate scientific questions. Through my coursework and research experiences, I have learned that collecting data is only one part of the scientific process. Researchers must also organize, analyze, and communicate their findings effectively.

My research interests include cardiovascular medicine, clinical data analysis, and public health. I am particularly interested in how computational techniques can be used to identify patterns in patient information and study differences in healthcare outcomes.

In DS4200, I have learned about different methods of visualizing information. Visualizations such as bar charts and scatterplots can make large amounts of information easier to understand. Choosing the correct type of visualization is important because different graphical methods communicate different characteristics of data.

For this website, I included a D3 bar chart using the cars dataset from our course materials. The dataset includes vehicle body styles, drive-wheel categories, and prices. The visualization compares prices across different vehicle types and drive-wheel categories.

Although the example uses car prices rather than healthcare data, it demonstrates a visualization technique that can be applied to many different subjects. For example, similar bar charts could compare disease prevalence between different populations or healthcare access across geographic areas.

My goal is to continue developing my data visualization skills and eventually apply these techniques to research involving medicine and public health. I want to create visualizations that are accurate, readable, and useful for understanding complex data.
